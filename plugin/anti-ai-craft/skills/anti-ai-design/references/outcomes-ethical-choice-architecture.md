## ADO-079 — Use transparent, reversible defaults

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** A plan, privacy, billing, quantity, communication, or configuration option will be preselected.

**Decision.** Choose the default that best serves the person's likely context, explain material consequences before commitment, and allow simple changes. Sensitive consent, additional spending, and data sharing require an affirmative choice when applicable.

**Evidence.** The team can justify the initial value through user benefit, test the alternative, and demonstrate that cost, renewal, and reversal remain visible.

**Avoid.** A box checked for commercial convenience, a costly option hidden from view, preselection that transfers data, or a default treated as universal authorization.

## ADO-080 — Direct attention without hiding alternatives

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** A decision has a recommended option, primary action, refusal, deferral, or lower-commitment alternative.

**Decision.** Use hierarchy to make the recommendation comprehensible while keeping relevant alternatives legible, reachable, and semantically correct. Differentiate destructive actions and preserve the possibility of refusal.

**Evidence.** The person identifies the recommendation, alternatives, and consequences through reading, keyboard, screen reader, and zoom without depending only on color or position.

**Avoid.** A disguised refusal button, an alternative renamed to shame, a destructive action with positive visual weight, or a single choice disguised as comparison.

## ADO-081 — Reduce effort without removing material information

**Strength.** default

**Apply when.** A flow presents excessive options, text, fields, or decisions before completion.

**Decision.** Remove what does not alter the task, defer recoverable detail, and divide genuine complexity into coherent stages. Keep price, renewal, risk, permission, limitation, and consequence at the moment they influence the choice.

**Evidence.** Every remaining element has a function; every removed element remains dispensable for informed decision-making, security, and recovery.

**Avoid.** Calling omission simplicity, hiding fees in a later stage, fragmenting the flow to impede comparison, or reducing accessibility in the name of speed.

## ADO-082 — Classify friction before removing or creating it

**Strength.** default

**Apply when.** The team intends to remove steps or add confirmation, qualification, review, authentication, or a statement of intent.

**Decision.** Classify friction as protective, necessary, declarative, administrative, or manipulative. Preserve friction that prevents error or supports a useful decision; simplify bureaucratic friction; remove manipulative friction.

**Evidence.** The flow records the risk avoided, data used, benefit to the person, and cost of each stage, along with an alternative for access needs.

**Avoid.** Treating fewer clicks as a law, forcing effort to create commitment, collecting data without a consumer, or adding obstacles merely to reduce abandonment.

## ADO-083 — Use urgency only when the limit is real

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** An interface communicates a deadline, availability, capacity, queue, inventory, or temporary condition.

**Decision.** Display only limits originating from verifiable state, update the information coherently, and explain what happens when the limit ends. Allow reasonable time to understand risky choices.

**Evidence.** The value has a source, timestamp, post-expiration behavior, and a test for divergence between the interface and authoritative system.

**Avoid.** A resetting countdown, fictional inventory, fabricated activity, permanent urgency, a message that pressures without a real consequence, or a timer that harms accessibility.

## ADO-084 — Make social proof verifiable and consensual

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** The interface uses testimonials, ratings, customer counts, logos, rankings, or cases.

**Decision.** Publish only authentic, authorized, contextualized, still-representative material. Distinguish opinion, observed result, average, sample, and example.

**Evidence.** There is an origin, consent, period, calculation method, and update or removal policy for every material proof point.

**Avoid.** A synthetic person presented as a customer, ratings selected to simulate unanimity, a logo without authorization, a number without a denominator, or an exceptional case treated as a typical outcome.

## ADO-085 — Explain losses without threatening or trapping

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** Cancellation, downgrade, expiration, deletion, or a plan change alters features, data, or collaboration.

**Decision.** Explain factually what remains, what changes, when it changes, and which legitimate options exist. Offer export, time, pause, or recovery when the contract permits, without making exit conditional on another purchase.

**Evidence.** The message matches actual behavior; the person can complete, return, export, and request help through accessible paths.

**Avoid.** An emotional threat, guilt, exaggerated loss, data held hostage, hidden cancellation, or an alternative that restarts billing without clear consent.

## ADO-086 — Reject mechanisms of harmful dependency

**Strength.** required

**Apply when.** A proposal is intended to increase return, usage time, habit, notifications, or switching cost.

**Decision.** Build continuity through recurring utility, trust, consensual collaboration, and portable accumulated value. Give people control over frequency, notifications, history, and exit.

**Evidence.** There is an explicit benefit to the person, accessible controls, well-being or harm metrics, and review for vulnerable audiences.

**Avoid.** An unpredictable reward to prolong use, excessive interruption, fear of losing a streak, an artificial migration barrier, exploitation of minors, or optimization for compulsion.
